## 2. Locked `.xls` workbooks

Excel encrypts a workbook with a password of its own, `VelvetSweatshop`, when it must encrypt one and is given none (a protected structure, a file saved to be opened read-only). Excel opens such a file without asking, so `xlsdown::open` now does too.

- `crates/xlsdown/src/crypt.rs` (new) and `crypt/tests.rs`.
  - `Lock::read(filepass)` reads the record that states the lock (`FILEPASS`, 0x002F): `Lock::Rc4` (kind 1, version 1.1; the key made by MD5) and `Lock::CryptoApi` (versions 2 to 4 with minor 2; the key made by SHA-1; RC4 and SHA-1 only). XOR hiding (kind 0), any other version, and a record cut short give `None`.
  - `Key::rc4`, `Key::crypto_api`, and `Key::stream_of(stretch)`: the 1024 bytes laid over one stretch of the stream. `Lock::key(password)` checks the verifier and gives the key only for the right password.
  - `unlocked(stream, password)`: `Ok(None)` when the workbook's own records hold no lock; the records decrypted when the password opens it; `UnsupportedFormat("an encrypted workbook")` otherwise. That message is unchanged, so counts of refusals stay comparable.
  - The rules of the format, which the tests pin: a fresh key every 1024 bytes of the stream, by the byte's place in the whole stream; the four bytes that head each record are not encrypted but their places in the key's stream are passed over; seven records are never encrypted (0x0809, 0x002F, 0x00E1, 0x0194, 0x0195, 0x0196, 0x0138); the first four bytes of the record that lists a sheet (0x0085) are not either.
  - `Rc4` is written there. `md-5` (already in the lock file) and `sha1` are new dependencies of `xlsdown`, declared in the root `Cargo.toml`.
- `crates/xlsdown/src/lib.rs`: `open` calls `crypt::unlocked(&stream, crypt::DEFAULT_PASSWORD)` before `globals::read`. `globals.rs` no longer refuses the record itself.
- `crates/xlsdown/src/testing.rs`: `Book::locked(password, Locking)` with `Locking::{Rc4, CryptoApi { key_bits }}`, a `lock_stated` that writes the record, and an `encrypt` written apart from the reader's `decrypt`.
- Tests: 5 in `crypt/tests.rs` and 2 in `tests.rs` (48 in `xlsdown`). The known answers came from a workbook LibreOffice locked: this repository's public `form.xlsx`, saved as `.xls` with the password, in scratch. Its salt and verifier, and three of its records past the first 1024 bytes, are constants in the test. I broke the code four ways on purpose (sheet positions encrypted, a stretch keyed as its neighbor, the salt mixed fifteen times, any password accepted) and each was caught.
- How to make such a file again: LibreOffice headless with a Basic macro in a scratch profile (`-env:UserInstallation=file://…`, `macro:///Standard.Module1.Main`, `storeToURL` with `FilterName` "MS Excel 97" and a `Password`). LibreOffice 7.3 writes the MD5 kind only.
- **The SHA-1 kind has no real file behind it.** Its tests lock and unlock workbooks built in code, which shows the two halves agree, not that Excel agrees. The verifier is checked first, so a wrong password cannot slip through; the risk is only in a real file whose layout I have misread, which would be refused or read wrongly. Say so if it ever comes up.
- Bindings: `crates/xlsdown-node/__test__/xls-test.ts` can lock a workbook (`ledger`, `lockedByExcel`, `lockedByItsOwner`; MD5 from `node:crypto`, RC4 written there). Specs in `index.spec.ts`, `wasi.spec.ts`, and `wasm/__test__/suite.ts`. READMEs and the constructor's doc comment say what is opened and what is refused.
- Not built: a `password` option, the XOR hiding, encrypted `.xlsx` (a different scheme, in `EncryptionInfo` and `EncryptedPackage` streams of a compound file, which `mimedown` already names), and locked `.doc` files.

## 4. After a retest on the other machine: the audit reads formulas properly

The user shared a second update from the work side (`BRAINSTORM/phase-two/reference/updateII.md`, private): the released readers scored against their system of record, with a list of where the formula reader loses. The fixes are general, in `xlsxdown`, and uncommitted.

- `crates/xlsxdown/src/audit/parse.rs` (new, with `parse/tests.rs`): a formula read into tokens and a small tree (`Expr`), and `shape(formula, references) -> Shape { adds, product }`. `adds` is the references the formula's value is the sum of, each with its sign; `product` is the two cells it multiplies. Both see through `ROUND(x, n)`, `TEXT(x, …)`, `VALUE`, `N`, `IFERROR(x, nothing)`, and an `IF` whose other answer shows nothing (text, a nought, or left out); `adds` also takes an `IF` whose two answers are the same sum, and a joining with `&` where one part is a sum. Anything not known (a name, an error, another function) makes both `None`. Nesting past 64 is given up on.
- `audit.rs` now builds each `Formula { reads, adds, product, value }` from that. `reads` is every number read (for reach, and for what counts as read); `adds` is what is followed. The old `workings`, `only_adds`, and `taken_away` are gone, and with them a wrong sign on `A5-SUM(B1:B3)`.
- A formula left with text that holds a digit is in the graph when it comes to a sum; its value is the sum of the cells it adds. `money()` takes such text as money when it reads as an amount (`reads_as_money`).
- `Graph::total(asked, steps)` ranks by: comes to the amount asked for, shown as money, reach, row, column, sheet. `Workbook::audit_toward(asked)` is public; `audit()` is `audit_toward(None)`; the tests' small budget goes through the private `audited(asked, steps)`.
- `Audit::shown_as_money` is new.
- `invoice.rs`: asks the formulas for the labels' total first; returns early for a total under half a cent, and for a total not shown as money beside a labeled amount that is (with a warning that has the words "not shown as money"); `as_shown` gives an amount as its cell's text spells it when that is the amount rounded to two or more places.
- Quantity and price: the one shown as money is the price; where the formats do not say, the factor that is a sum is the quantity.
- `format.rs`, `language`: the hex digits past the fourth are the calendar and the digits; the everyday calendar (0, 1, 2) and plain digits (0, 1) are accepted.
- Every new rule's test was broken on purpose and seen to fail (seven mutations, all caught).
- On the local workbooks afterwards (figures in chat only): both workbooks the update names as failing now give a total the labels agree with, and items.

Not done from that update, on purpose or for later:
- Two items that cancel (a charge and its credit) are both on the sheet, so both stay. It is the caller's choice to drop them.
- Elapsed time (`[h]:mm:ss`), and conditions with digits spread across literals (the phone-number format).
- A line that is hours in all times a rate, with no per-row amounts on the sheet, is one item described by its own row. Their system holds the task's name. Making an item for each row of hours would mean amounts no cell holds.
- The label reader in `contentdown` takes seconds on a sheet of a hundred thousand cells and more (a list, not an invoice); it is older than this work, and the audit is not where the time goes.
