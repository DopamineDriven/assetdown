## 3. A workbook's total comes from its formulas, and the labels never choose

`0419.md`, section 4, describes a rule I added: the formulas were asked first for one that came to the amount the labels on the page gave. It was released, scored on the other machine against the system of record, and made amounts worse. The user: "we should just use the formulas."

- How I found which rule it was: every workbook of the archive is on this machine now (section 4), so I ran all of them with each of the three new rules switched on and off, through a temporary probe test and environment switches, one line per file into the ignored private folder. The label rule alone moved the totals; the other two moved almost nothing. The probe and the switches are removed again.
- The fix, in `crates/xlsxdown/src/audit.rs`: `Graph::total(steps)` ranks by shown as money, then cells reached, then row, column, and sheet. `Workbook::audit_toward` is gone. `invoice` calls `audit()`.
- Kept from that release, because they are about reading formulas and not about trusting labels: the parser (`audit/parse.rs`), totals written out as text, amounts as their cells show them, the quantity that is a sum, a total under half a cent leaving the labels' reading alone, and the guard for a formula total shown as a plain number beside a labeled amount shown as money.
- Tests: `what_the_labels_read_does_not_choose_among_the_formulas` in `invoice/tests.rs` replaces the test that pinned the removed rule, and was watched failing against it first.
- **The lesson, which is also a memory file (`workbook-totals-formulas-first`):** before changing how a field is chosen, run the change over the whole local corpus with the rule on and off and count what moves. I shipped that rule on the strength of two named workbooks and counts that could not tell a right total from a wrong one.
- What I cannot do here: score. The system's own amounts are on the other machine. An export of id and amount into the ignored private folder would let a change be scored before it ships.
